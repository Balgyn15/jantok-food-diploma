# Literature review — evidence-informed draft

Prepared 22 October 2026 for JANTOK-3. This is a desk review for Ayana and the supervisor to check; it is not evidence of Ayana's own reading or approval. The scope is household meal selection, dietary constraints, explainability, and evaluation. Scientific sources are linked to publisher/PubMed records. Findings from other countries and clinical populations are not assumed to generalize to Astana families.

## Structured source analysis

| # | Publication / source | Type and method | Relevant result | Limit and use in JANTOK |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | [Saad & Islam, 2025, *Computers in Biology and Medicine*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40328030/) | Scientific scoping review; 36 of 166 screened studies | Food classification/recommendation approaches vary widely. | Heterogeneous methods and outcomes prevent choosing an algorithm by headline accuracy. Start with auditable constraints and compare later recommendations against a baseline. |
| 2 | [Yera et al., 2023, *IJERPH*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36901271/) | Scientific systematic review of food recommenders for diabetes | Reviews algorithms and evaluation practices for a restricted diet. | Disease-specific evidence cannot validate general family advice. Use it to define transparent constraint tests, not medical claims. |
| 3 | [Vandeputte et al., 2023, *Journal of Nutrition*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36894251/) | Scientific algorithm study plus online tests (255 adults) and app study (27 adults) | User participation in choosing substitutions improved acceptance in the reported study. | Small app sample and a food-swap setting. Offer an explained, selectable replacement rather than silently changing a meal. |
| 4 | [De Croon et al., 2025, *JMIR mHealth and uHealth*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39935009/) | Scientific user-centered workplace deployment; interviews/prototypes/proof of principle | Explanations helped understanding and perceived control; attrition was 77% after seven weeks. | Workplace catering is not a home subscription and attrition limits long-term claims. Show why a meal is offered or blocked; measure comprehension and retention separately. |
| 5 | [*Towards interactive explanation-based nutrition virtual coaching systems*, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38261966/) | Scientific interactive-system experiment | Participants valued feedback and explanations about alternatives. | Virtual-coach setting differs from order fulfillment. Prototype a concise explanation and collect task-level feedback. |
| 6 | [Sun et al., 2023, *Journal of Medical Internet Research*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36656630/) | Scientific systematic scoping review of health recommender systems | Only 10 of 51 included systems considered end-user preferences in design/development. | Broad health domain; no proof that JANTOK users have the same priorities. Include users in requirement validation and usability testing. |
| 7 | [*Effects of a personalized nutrition program on cardiometabolic health*, 2024, *Nature Medicine*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38714898/) | Scientific randomized trial, 347 adults, 18-week app intervention | Triglycerides and some secondary outcomes improved; LDL cholesterol did not significantly differ. | Biomarker-heavy intervention and declared commercial ties; JANTOK cannot claim similar health outcomes. Evaluate usable workflows and constraint compliance instead. |
| 8 | [*Effect of a Personalized Diet… vs a Low-fat Diet*, 2022, *JAMA Network Open*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36169954/) | Scientific randomized trial, 199 analyzed | Personalized diet did not yield significantly greater weight loss at six months. | Population had abnormal glucose metabolism/obesity. This counterexample prevents claiming personalization is inherently better. |
| 9 | [Shin et al., 2025, *American Journal of Preventive Medicine*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40120994/) | Scientific randomized online-grocery trial | A combined labeling, feedback, ordering and substitution intervention improved cart nutrition score in that setting. | Cannot isolate the effect of substitutions; shopping is not prepared-meal delivery. Compare JANTOK recommendations with a simple unfiltered menu baseline. |
| 10 | [*Health-Aware Food Recommendation Based on Knowledge Graph and Multi-Task Learning*, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37238897/) | Scientific algorithm/model evaluation | Models preference and health-related factors jointly. | Offline recommendation metrics do not establish allergen or clinical safety. Keep hard exclusions separate from ranking. |
| 11 | [WHO, Healthy diet, 2026](https://www.who.int/en/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet) | Public-health guidance, not a project user study | Adequacy, balance, moderation and diversity are broad principles. | Do not convert population guidance into personal medical prescriptions; use it as background and require professional review of nutrition rules. |
| 12 | [US FDA, Food Allergies](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/food-allergies?lv=true) | Official food-safety guidance, not local regulation | Cross-contact and undeclared allergens remain risks even when ingredient lists exist. | A software filter is only one control. Require ingredient provenance, kitchen process review and prominent uncertainty warnings before real use. |

**Count:** 12 meaningfully used sources; 10 scientific publications, all dated 2022–2025. WHO/FDA are context and safety sources, not substitutes for research or applicable Kazakhstan requirements.

## Synthesis and design implications

1. Preference ranking and allergy exclusion solve different problems. A deterministic exclusion check should run before any preference score (sources 2, 10, 12). A blocked item must show a reason and never be offered as a selectable alternative.
2. Explanations and user choice have promising evidence, but the studies are context-specific and may have high attrition (sources 3–6). The prototype shows meal details, replacement options and an allergy conflict; usability sessions must test whether people understand them.
3. Personalized nutrition has mixed outcome evidence (sources 7–9). The diploma aim should be reliable family planning and traceable fulfillment, with operational and usability metrics, rather than an unproven health benefit.
4. A source does not validate the local problem. The 3–5 representative discussions or actual-client agreement required by JANTOK-2 are still pending. Findings from those sessions should revise requirements and this review.
