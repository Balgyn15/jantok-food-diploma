# Literature review — version 1.0

Updated 23 October 2026. YouTrack: JANTOK-3. Scope: household meal selection, dietary constraints, explanations and evaluation. This targeted critical review uses 12 sources, including 10 scientific publications from 2022–2025 and two official context sources. It informs requirements and evaluation; it does not establish local demand or clinical benefit.

Bibliographic metadata and abstracts for all 10 scientific records were checked against the NCBI PubMed record on 23 October 2026. Open full-text discussion/limitations were also checked for L02/L04/L05/L06/L07/L08/L10; L01/L03/L09 are bounded by the available record/abstract. No complete systematic search is claimed. Source IDs L01–L12 correspond to table rows 1–12 and the bibliography below.

| 3 | [Vandeputte et al., 2023, *Journal of Nutrition*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36894251/) | Scientific algorithm study plus online tests (255 adults) and app study (27 adults) | User participation in choosing substitutions improved acceptance in the reported study. | Small app sample and a food-swap setting. Offer an explained, selectable replacement rather than silently changing a meal. |
| 4 | [De Croon et al., 2025, *JMIR mHealth and uHealth*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39935009/) | Scientific user-centered workplace deployment; interviews/prototypes/proof of principle | Explanations helped understanding and perceived control; attrition was 77% after seven weeks. | Self-selected users, mixed real/virtual orders and high attrition limit generalization. Workplace catering differs from family delivery. Show exclusion reasons; measure comprehension and retention separately. |
| 5 | [*Towards interactive explanation-based nutrition virtual coaching systems*, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38261966/) | Scientific interactive-system experiment | Participants valued feedback and explanations about alternatives. | Explanation and feedback were introduced together, so their separate effects cannot be isolated. Virtual coaching differs from fulfillment. Prototype concise reasons and collect task-level feedback. |
| 6 | [Sun et al., 2023, *Journal of Medical Internet Research*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36656630/) | Scientific systematic scoping review of health recommender systems | Only 10 of 51 included systems considered end-user preferences in design/development. | Language/search restrictions may omit studies; the scoping review does not determine outcome utility. Broad health evidence does not establish Astana priorities. Include users in requirement validation. |
| 7 | [*Effects of a personalized nutrition program on cardiometabolic health*, 2024, *Nature Medicine*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38714898/) | Scientific randomized trial, 347 adults, 18-week app intervention | Triglycerides and some secondary outcomes improved; LDL cholesterol did not significantly differ. | Biomarker-heavy intervention and declared commercial ties; JANTOK cannot claim similar health outcomes. Evaluate usable workflows and constraint compliance instead. |
| 8 | [*Effect of a Personalized Diet… vs a Low-fat Diet*, 2022, *JAMA Network Open*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36169954/) | Scientific randomized trial, 199 analyzed | Personalized diet did not yield significantly greater weight loss at six months. | Single-system, English-speaking sample, limited retention and pandemic disruption constrain generalization. Clinical participants differ from JANTOK users; personalization cannot be presumed superior. |
| 9 | [Shin et al., 2025, *American Journal of Preventive Medicine*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40120994/) | Scientific randomized online-grocery trial | A combined labeling, feedback, ordering and substitution intervention improved cart nutrition score in that setting. | Cannot isolate the effect of substitutions; shopping is not prepared-meal delivery. Compare JANTOK recommendations with a simple unfiltered menu baseline. |
| 10 | [*Health-Aware Food Recommendation Based on Knowledge Graph and Multi-Task Learning*, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37238897/) | Scientific algorithm/model evaluation | Models preference and health-related factors jointly. | One Allrecipes dataset, four nutrient dimensions and no user study limit evidence. Allergies are suggested as future work, not a verified capability. Keep hard exclusions separate from ranking. |
| 11 | [WHO, Healthy diet, 2026](https://www.who.int/en/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet) | Public-health guidance, not a project user study | Adequacy, balance, moderation and diversity are broad principles. | Do not convert population guidance into personal medical prescriptions; use it as background and require professional review of nutrition rules. |
| 12 | [US FDA, Food Allergies](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/food-allergies) | Official food-safety guidance, not local regulation | Cross-contact and undeclared allergens remain risks even when ingredient lists exist. | A software filter is only one control. Require ingredient provenance, kitchen process review and prominent uncertainty warnings before real use. |

**Count:** 12 meaningfully used sources; 10 scientific publications, all dated 2022–2025. WHO/FDA are context and safety sources, not substitutes for research or applicable Kazakhstan requirements.

## Synthesis and design implications

1. Preference ranking and allergy exclusion solve different problems. A deterministic exclusion check should run before any preference score (sources 2, 10, 12). A blocked item must show a reason and never be offered as a selectable alternative.
2. Explanations and user choice have promising evidence, but the studies are context-specific and may have high attrition (sources 3–6). The prototype shows meal details, replacement options and an allergy conflict; usability sessions must test whether people understand them.
3. Personalized nutrition has mixed outcome evidence (sources 7–9). The diploma aim should be reliable family planning and traceable fulfillment, with operational and usability metrics, rather than an unproven health benefit.
4. A source does not validate the local problem. The [anonymized P01–P06 notes](RELEVANCE_EVIDENCE_SUPPLEMENT.md) document six conversations reported by the project owner and add local problem observations, including counterexamples. The original conversations and consent records have not been independently checked; the small group does not establish prevalence or demand. Its findings should be tested with users and used to revise requirements and this review when warranted.

## Search and appraisal method

Searches on 22–23 October 2026 used PubMed/publisher/official records with food recommender, personalized nutrition, explanation, allergy, digital intervention and randomized trial. Selection favored recent scientific work with an explicit design implication; WHO/FDA supplement context. Clinical studies were retained as counter-evidence to exaggerated personalization claims, not as a recipe for the service. Studies with unrelated populations are explicitly limited in the table.

The search is targeted and does not claim systematic-review completeness. Full-text discussion/limitation checks strengthen appraisal for seven sources; the three record/abstract-only sources cannot establish detailed implementation or bias claims. Further full-text reading and source changes should be recorded as subsequent review work. WHO/FDA do not replace applicable Kazakhstan requirements or kitchen expertise. Each source is meaningfully used in the table/synthesis rather than counted solely as a link.
