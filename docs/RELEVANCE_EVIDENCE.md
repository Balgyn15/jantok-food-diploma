# Relevance evidence — version 1.0

Date: 23 October 2026. Lead: Ayana. YouTrack: JANTOK-2.

## Evidence collected so far

The team confirms four informal conversations with acquaintances, approximately 25–40 years old. The initial team account describes working and/or married acquaintances; individual employment/family details were not retained. On 23 October 2026 the team recalled generally positive reactions from all four to the service idea. No recording or contemporaneous participant-level notes were preserved; original discussion dates and detailed individual answers remain unknown. This is a retrospective account, with no verbatim quotations or invented participant characteristics.

The recalled theme is the effort of deciding on and preparing meals after work. Ready-made family meals could make it easier for household members to share or outsource that recurring task. The team also sees potential to reduce disagreements about who cooks; this social effect has not been measured and cannot be promised.

## Short follow-up for the four actual representatives

Ask what they currently do for meals after work, one recent difficulty, how often it occurs, what they would expect for different household members, and what could prevent adoption (price, ingredients, delivery, trust). Ask whether they would use the service rather than only whether they like the idea. Record the real follow-up date, a paraphrase of the answer, any contrary view and a linked requirement/decision. This is a follow-up guide, not a set of interview results.

## Decisions supported now

- Define the target group as working households in Astana; investigate meal coordination as a concrete task.
- Keep separate member profiles and a visible subscription quote in the design, then test whether these solve the reported task.
- Compare with local services; a claim of no Kazakhstan/Astana competitors is not supported.
- Treat household conflict reduction, demand size, preferred price and willingness to subscribe as questions for follow-up.
